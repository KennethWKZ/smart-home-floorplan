For the installer

  1. One supply per strip. Keep each supply under the load limit above for continuous use. When a strip goes over, the Supply column names the size to fit instead; past 600 W, split it across two supplies and drivers.
  2. Long runs get fed at both ends. Run every wire from the driver's output (V+ and each colour channel) to each feed point. A power-only loop from the supply leaves the far end without colour control.
  3. One power point per strip. Each strip has one 24 V supply and one driver. The Gaming ceiling, Living TV and headboard strips are closed rectangles: their ends meet at one corner, so feeding both ends is a short jumper there. The mirror strip is open and 6.3 m long, over the 5 m a single feed should carry, so its far end gets a second wire pair from the same driver, not a second supply. If only one end is wired, measure the far end with the strip on full white: 23.3 V or more is fine.
  4. Wires on site (checked by you). The three 3-tone COB strips have 3-wire cables (24 V common, warm, cool): tunable white, so set each driver to dual colour-temperature mode. The Gaming strip has 5 wires (24 V common, red, green, blue, white): set its driver to RGBW mode. Wire every strip straight to the Aqara driver, not to a remote-control box sold with it.
  5. Ask for the feeder wire size. The 24 V common wire carries the whole strip's current: up to about 7 A on the Gaming strip at full white, about 5 A on the Living TV strip. At 1 mm² or thicker, a 3 m feeder loses about 2% or less; at 0.5 mm² it's about double. The far-end voltage check below settles it either way.
  6. Driver limit: the Aqara Smart Light Strip Driver handles up to 288 W at 24 V, per the Malaysian reseller's listing (not confirmed on Aqara's own spec page). Every strip here is well under that.
  7. Feeders already installed? Then skip the cable inputs and measure instead: with the strip on full white, read the voltage at its far end. 23.3 V or more means the drop is under 3%.
  8. Supplies: 24 V output to match the strips, mounted where they can breathe and be reached for service, not sealed behind plaster. Mains wiring by a registered electrician.

Voltage drop uses copper at 0.0175 Ω·mm²/m over the two-wire feeder, with each feed point carrying an equal share of the current, and treats 3% at the strip as the limit. Drop along the strip itself is covered by the run-per-feed figure from the strip's datasheet. Your entries stay in this browser.
